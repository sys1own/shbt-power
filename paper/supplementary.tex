\section{Graser Driver Macro-Burst Timing Derivation}
The primary specification requires a time-averaged optical beam power of
$P_{\mathrm{avg}} = 25.0\MW$ at a macro-pulse repetition frequency of
$f_{\mathrm{rep}} = 100.0\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Energy conservation fixes the
optical energy delivered per macro-shot:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{macro}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{avg}}}{f_{\mathrm{rep}}}
 = \frac{25.0\times10^{6}}{100.0} = 250.0\kJ.
\label{eq:emacro}
\end{equation}
The driver also specifies a constituent micro-pulse of
$E_{\mathrm{micro}} = 100.0\,\mathrm{J}$ in a coherence window
$\tau_{\mathrm{micro}} = 1.0\ps$, i.e.\ an instantaneous peak of
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{peak}} = \frac{E_{\mathrm{micro}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{micro}}}
 = \frac{100.0\,\mathrm{J}}{1.0\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{s}} = 100.0\TW.
\label{eq:ppeak}
\end{equation}
Treating \eqref{eq:ppeak} as a $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$ isolated shot would
under-drive the core by a factor of $2{,}500$ ($10\,\mathrm{kW}$ average),
while concentrating \eqref{eq:emacro} into a single $1\ps$ envelope would
demand $2.5\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{W}$, beyond any dielectric breakdown
threshold and deep into relativistic self-focusing
\cite{mourou2018,plasma_mirrors2018}. The contradiction is resolved by an
RF bunch hierarchy on a C-band linac at
$f_{\mathrm{rf}} = 5.712\GHz$:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{rf}} = \frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{rf}}}
 = 1.750700\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{s} = 175.070\ps.
\end{equation}
The micro-bunch count per macro-shot is
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{micro}} = \frac{E_{\mathrm{macro}}}{E_{\mathrm{micro}}}
 = \frac{250{,}000}{100} = 2{,}500.
\end{equation}
Under synchronous fundamental-bucket filling ($h=1$) the burst duration is
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{burst}} = N_{\mathrm{micro}} T_{\mathrm{rf}}
 = 2500\times 175.070\ps = 437.675\ns,
\end{equation}
(875.350 ns for every-second-bucket filling $h=2$), both comfortably inside
the $1.0\mus$ flat-top of cryogenic C-band klystrons \cite{bes2009}. The
intermediate burst power is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{burst}} = \frac{E_{\mathrm{macro}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{burst}}}
 = \frac{250.0\times10^{3}}{4.37675\times10^{-7}}
 \approx 571.2\GW,
\end{equation}
with macro duty factor
$D_{\mathrm{macro}} = \tau_{\mathrm{burst}} f_{\mathrm{rep}}
 = 4.37675\times10^{-5}$ and a quiescent cooling interval of
$\approx 9.99956\ms$ between bursts. Peak power is set by the 1 ps
micro-pulse (100 TW), intermediate power by the 437.675 ns burst envelope
(571.2 GW), and average power by the 100 Hz repetition (25.0 MW)
\cite{campri2022,mdpi_fel2022,muggli2021,bnl_atf2020}.

\section{Injector Trade Study: Light-Gas versus Lorentz Railgun}
\label{sec:injector}
Two launch architectures were evaluated for delivering the 3.71 mg pellet
to the chamber target point at $v_{\mathrm{inj}} = 250\,\mathrm{m/s}$:

\textbf{(a) Two-stage light-gas pneumatic injector.} A helium/hydrogen
driver-gas stage accelerates a carrier sabot down a smooth-bore barrel.
Advantages: gentle acceleration over long stroke, mature cryogenic pellet
handling heritage, high reliability. Disadvantages: the discardable sabot
must be split and diverted before the vacuum port (adding debris-handling
hardware and a separation nose cone); driver-gas leakage through the
barrel/chamber boundary seal raises background pressure and parasitic load
on the cryopumps; gas-dynamic flow at the muzzle perturbs pellet
trajectory at the $\pm5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ transverse level; cycle time at
100 Hz leaves little margin for recharging the driver reservoir.

\textbf{(b) Electromagnetic Lorentz railgun (adopted).} A sabot-free
conducting armature couples Lorentz force directly to the pellet carrier:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F} = I\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{B}, \qquad
a(t) = \frac{\mathcal{L}'I(t)^2}{2m_{\mathrm{carrier}}},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{L}'$ the per-unit-length rail inductance. Advantages: no
driver gas (the vacuum boundary seal is maintained), no sabot discard
stage, millisecond-scale electronic timing synchronizable with the graser
trigger gate, and position feedback from the same rail sensors. The chosen
fifth-order minimum-jerk acceleration schedule
\begin{equation}
s(\tau) = 10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5, \qquad \tau \in [0,1],
\label{eq:minjerk}
\end{equation}
normalizes displacement along the launch stroke with vanishing velocity,
acceleration, and jerk at both endpoints, minimizing pellet stress and
trajectory dispersion at handoff to the $2.50\m$ free-flight corridor.

Muzzle exit at $v_{\mathrm{inj}} = 250\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over the
$L_{\mathrm{flight}} = 2.50\m$ standoff gives an in-chamber transit time of
exactly $10.0\ms$, synchronizing single-pellet transit to the 100 Hz
repetition. Quadrant photodiode laser gates bound transverse position to
$\pm5.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$; an optical arrival gate closes the graser trigger
within $\pm50.0\ps$ \cite{hamamatsu2020}.

\section{Wakefield Green's Functions and Slippage Integrals}
\label{app:wakefields}
The bunch-to-bunch longitudinal wake of the C-band structure is
generated from the Green's function
\begin{equation}
W_z(\tau) = 2\,k_{\mathrm{loss}}\,e^{-\omega_r \tau / (2Q_{\mathrm{ext}})}
\cos\!\left(\omega_r\tau\sqrt{1-\tfrac{1}{4Q_{\mathrm{ext}}^2}}\right)
\end{equation}
for $\tau > 0$ with $W_z(0^{+}) = k_{\mathrm{loss}}$. The
accumulated voltage seen by bunch $n$ is the causal convolution
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_n = -\sum_{j < n} q_b\,W_z\!\left((n-j)\,T_b\right),
\end{equation}
evaluated over a sliding window of $W = 32$ bunches ($O(N\times W)$,
allocation-free) since the choke decay $\tau_d =
2Q_{\mathrm{ext}}/\omega_{\mathrm{HOM}} < 5.5\,\mathrm{ns}$ ($\sim$30
bunch intervals at $T_b = 175.070\,\mathrm{ps}$) truncates the
series. The optical-klystron slippage per stage is
\begin{equation}

= N_u\,\lambda_r,
\end{equation}
so the emitted radiation slips by integer resonant wavelengths per
undulator period; the LLRF feedforward residual
$\Delta\gamma/\gamma = \epsilon_{\mathrm{ff}}\cdot\mathrm{droop}
\le 10^{-4}$ bounds the associated energy modulation.

\section{Discretized Solver Algorithms}
\label{app:algorithms}
\textbf{Cavity envelope step.} Each bunch applies a semi-implicit Euler
update to the driven cavity-envelope equation of the main text:
$v^{n+1} = v^n + \Delta t\,[\omega_{1/2}(u^n - v^n) - R_a I_b^n/(2Q_L)]$,
$\Delta t = T_b = 175.070\ps$, with feedforward $u^n$ canceling beam
pull; phase is extracted as $\delta\phi_c = \arctan(\Im v / \Re v)$.

\textbf{Colebrook--White iteration.} The micro-channel Darcy factor is
solved by fixed-point iteration on
$1/\sqrt{f} = -2\log_{10}(\varepsilon/3.7D_h + 2.51/(\mathrm{Re}\sqrt{f}))$
from the Blasius seed $f_0 = 0.3164\,\mathrm{Re}^{-1/4}$, converged to
$|f^{k+1}-f^k| < 10^{-12}$ in fewer than 10 iterations.

\textbf{Axisymmetric sheath relaxation.} The Poisson residual
$\nabla^2\phi = -\rho_q/\epsilon_0$ is relaxed by Jacobi sweeps
$\phi \leftarrow \phi - \tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla^2\phi +
\rho_q/\epsilon_0)(d r\,d z)^2$ on a fixed $(n_z \times n_r)$ stencil
with caller-supplied buffers---no heap allocation inside the $100\,
\mathrm{Hz}$ loop---until the peak potential drops below the $45\,
\mathrm{eV}$ flashover barrier.

\textbf{Strain--life bisection.} Armor fatigue solves
$\frac{\Delta\varepsilon}{2} =
\frac{\sigma_f'}{E}(2N_f)^b + \varepsilon_f'(2N_f)^c$
by bisection on $N_f \in [1, 10^{10}]$; with $\Delta\varepsilon_p =
4.120\times10^{-4}$, $\Delta\varepsilon_e = 1.050\times10^{-3}$,
$\sigma_f' = 1.161\,\mathrm{GPa}$, $b = -0.09$, $c = -0.58$ the root is
$N_f = 4.382\times10^{6}$ cycles.

\textbf{ROM state step.} $\mathbf{x}_r^{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_r^n +
\Delta t\,[\mathbf{A}_0\mathbf{x}_r^n + \textstyle\sum_i
\theta_i\mathbf{A}_i\mathbf{x}_r^n + \mathbf{B}_0\mathbf{u}^n]$ over
fixed $12\times12$/$12\times4$ stack arrays in $4.20\mus$.



\textbf{Boris relativistic pusher.} Sheath macro-particles advance
with the Boris rotation: $\mathbf{p}^{-} = \mathbf{p}^n +
q\mathbf{E}\Delta t/2$; rotation by
$\mathbf{t} = q\mathbf{B}\Delta t/(2m\gamma)$,
$\mathbf{s} = 2\mathbf{t}/(1+t^2)$; then $\mathbf{p}^{n+1} =
\mathbf{p}^{+} + q\mathbf{E}\Delta t/2$ with
$\gamma = \sqrt{1+p^2/(mc)^2}$ --- energy-conserving to roundoff over
the $2\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ pulse.

\textbf{Cloud-in-cell deposition.} Each particle weight distributes
to the two bracketing nodes with linear fractions $(1-f, f)$,
$f = x/\Delta x - \lfloor x/\Delta x\rfloor$; the density grid is a
caller-owned fixed buffer.

\textbf{Geometric multigrid Poisson.} The sheath Poisson solve
nests V-cycles on the axisymmetric $(r,z)$ stencil with
red--black Gauss--Seidel smoothing and full-weighting restriction;
the residual is converged against $\epsilon_0\phi/\rho_q L^2 <
10^{-6}$ before each $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$ macro-tick commits.

\section{Calibration Traceability, Operando Diagnostics, and the
  Commissioning Stage-Gate Matrix}
\label{app:commissioning}
The Ni80Cr20 Joule calibration reference is a $R_{\mathrm{cal}} =
50.000 \pm 0.005\,\Omega$ resistor ($\alpha = 1.05\times10^{-4}$
K$^{-1}$, $\beta = -4.50\times10^{-8}$ K$^{-2}$, $R_{\mathrm{lead}}
\approx 0.15\,\Omega$, $Z_{\mathrm{in}} > 10$ G$\Omega$) driven
through microkernel MMIO registers. The weighted-least-squares
calorimetric line $Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 P_{\mathrm{cal},i} +
\varepsilon_i$ is certified at $|\hat{\beta}_1 - 1| \le 0.0020$ with
$u(\hat{\beta}_0) \le 0.15$ MW across five $10$--$50$ MW tiers held
$45$ min each; the GUM budget closes at $\sigma_b = 1.3416$ MW
($\Sigma = 1.8000$ MW$^2$) and $U_{95} = \pm 2.683$ MW ($\pm 0.202\%$,
$k=2$).

\paragraph{Operando diagnostic bounds}
64-channel Faraday array $\Gamma \le -32$ dB at $2.5$ GHz; suppressor
grids $-1.50$ kV, leakage $<10\,\mu$A; Thomson parabola $E_x = 1.20$
MV/m, $B_x = 0.85$ T, $\pm 25\,\mu$m; scCVD dark current $<10$ pA at
500 V/500 K; streak sweep $50$ mm/ps, jitter $\le 150$ fs; GaP EO
sampling $\le 1.0$ ps; BPM $\le 10\,\mu$m ($\pm 5\,\mu$m); CDR
$\ge 15\,\mu$m; telemetry frames at 10 kHz.

\paragraph{Stage-gate matrix}
Stage 1 vacuum $\le 10^{-5}$ Pa, leak $\le 10^{-10}$
Pa\,m$^3$/s, SECDED trap; Stage 2 $12.5$ MPa proof hold
$<0.005$ MPa/24h, $\Delta P_{\mathrm{core}} \le 0.3$ MPa, ASTM E1681
($K_{\mathrm{IEAC}} \ge 45$ MPa$\sqrt{\mathrm{m}}$) and F1624
($P_{\mathrm{th}}/P_{\mathrm{FFS}} \ge 0.75$); Stage 3 WLS gain
$|\hat{\beta}_1 - 1| \le 0.0020$; Stage 4 diagnostics linear at
24.14 MA; Stage 5 $\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{th}} = 1{,}325.0 \pm 2.68$ MW,
twin residual $\le 1.80$ MW/100 h.

\paragraph{SECDED telemetry driver}
\texttt{read\_validated\_telemetry(channel\_idx, out)} in
\texttt{kernel/src/shbt\_power\_kernel.c} performs the Hamming(72,64)
syndrome check inline: single-bit errors are corrected in place and
flagged; a double-bit detection raises \texttt{global\_fault\_irq}
and executes \texttt{cli; hlt} inside the 20 ns interlock budget, then
the raw payload is descaled to physical units per channel.
